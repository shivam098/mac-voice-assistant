**Test build — not yet tested on a real Mac.** The first build of Jarvis for Mac, for trying it on a Mac and saying
what breaks. Until now it has been built and tested only on GitHub's Macs, which have no microphone, no keyboard and
nobody to give permissions, so expect rough edges.

## What Jarvis does

Jarvis for Mac (the app is called Voice Assistant) lets you use your Mac by voice:

- **"Hey Jarvis"** opens and switches apps, starts typing in the right box and sends, opens and closes tabs, scrolls,
  and clicks buttons and links by name or by number: "Hey Jarvis, open Notes", "new tab", "scroll down".
- **Dictation anywhere:** tap Fn (🌐), speak, and tap it again. Your words are typed where you're typing, cleaned up and
  punctuated, in English or in Hindi + English.
- **An idea board and reminders:** "add renew passport to my to-do list", "remind me to call mom tomorrow at 5 pm".
- **An optional AI brain** for requests the commands don't cover. It runs on your Mac, and asks before it sends,
  deletes or closes anything.

Speech is recognised on your Mac, and nothing you say is uploaded. There's no account, and the logs never contain your
words.

## What's included

- `VoiceAssistant-0.1.0.dmg`: Voice Assistant.app, for Apple silicon and Intel Macs with macOS 13 or later.
- `SHA256SUMS`: the .dmg's SHA-256, to check your download.

The first time it runs, it downloads its speech models (about 265 MB). The AI brain is a separate 2.4 GB download, only
if you ask for it in Settings.

It's built by CI: the Release workflow built this `.dmg` on GitHub's macOS 15 runner, with Xcode 16.4, from the commit
tagged v0.1.0. To build it from source, you need Xcode 16.4 or later: CI builds with Xcode 16.4, and the owner's Mac
with Xcode 26.6 (Swift 6.3.3).

## Known limits

- **It isn't notarized.** It's signed ad hoc, not by Apple, so macOS blocks it the first time: open it once, then choose
  System Settings › Privacy & Security › **Open Anyway**. Each new test build needs Accessibility given again.
- **The AI brain needs Apple silicon and macOS 13.3 or later.** Intel Macs aren't offered it yet.
- **The AI brain's speed on a real Mac isn't known yet.** On GitHub's Macs it got 21 of its 25 test requests right
  (Windows gets 22), but they have no graphics chip it can use, so how fast it is and how much memory it takes on a
  real Mac are still to be measured.
- **Languages:** commands are in English; dictation is in English, or in Hindi + English (Hindi typed in Roman letters).
- **ThumbFree for Mac** uses Fn too: run one of the two at a time. Each warns while the other is running.

## Install

Follow [the testing guide](https://github.com/shivam098/mac-voice-assistant/blob/v0.1.0/docs/testing.md): download,
check the download, install, open it the first time, give it its permissions, then go through the checklist.

## Check the download

Put `VoiceAssistant-0.1.0.dmg` and `SHA256SUMS` in the same folder (Downloads, say), then in Terminal:

```sh
cd ~/Downloads
shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS
```

It should say `VoiceAssistant-0.1.0.dmg: OK`. If it says anything else, don't open the .dmg: download it again.

## Report a problem

Open an [issue](https://github.com/shivam098/mac-voice-assistant/issues) with what you did, what you expected and what
happened, your Mac's model, chip (Apple silicon or Intel) and macOS version, and the latest log from
`~/Library/Logs/VoiceAssistant` (logs never contain your words). Don't attach `board.json` or `learned.json`: they hold
your notes and phrases you've said. The testing guide's "If something goes wrong" has more.
